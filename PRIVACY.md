# ihz.land privacy

ihz.land is a personal fork of [Oyenza Studio](https://github.com/lucacadalora/oyenza-studio). It has no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and needs no account. Unless you sign in to ihz.app, it never sends anything about you to the fork's owner. On its own it reads one public file, to see whether a new version is out.

## What leaves your PC

**Lyrics lookups.** When a song plays and **Synced lyrics** is on, ihz.land asks [LRCLIB](https://lrclib.net), a free public lyrics library, for that song's lyrics. The request carries the song's title, artist, album and length, plus a User-Agent that names ihz.land and links to this project. LRCLIB is run by other people and has its own terms. Answers are cached on your PC, so a song isn't looked up again every time it plays.

To stop lookups, right-click the island or the tray icon and turn off **Synced lyrics**.

**Apple Music lyrics, only if you turn them on.** If you add your Apple Music token (right-click the island, then **Apple Music lyrics › Use token from clipboard**), ihz.land also asks Apple Music for lyrics the way Apple's web player does. It checks the token, searches Apple Music's catalog for the playing song's artist and title, and downloads that song's lyrics. These requests go to Apple (`music.apple.com` and `amp-api.music.apple.com`) and carry your token; the token goes nowhere else. To stop, choose **Remove token** in the same menu.

**Phone drop, only while its panel is open.** When you open Phone drop on the Shelf, ihz.land serves a small web page on your local network so your phone can send files to the Shelf and save Shelf files. Only a device on the same network with that session's secret link, which only the QR code carries, can open it. Files go straight between your phone and your PC: nothing passes through the internet or through us. ihz.land reads nothing from an upload until you click **Accept**. The page stops working when you click **Done**, or after 10 minutes without a phone.

**Paired phones, only after you pair one.** **Pair a phone** (Shelf › Phone) shows a QR code with a pairing secret made on your PC. The phone that scans it keeps the secret, and so does your PC, sealed so only your Windows account can read it. From then on, while ihz.land runs, it keeps one request open at the fork's small meeting server, `drop.ihz.app`, so the phone can find the PC from any network. The server sees your PC's and phone's IP addresses, when they are online, and an address for the pair that is derived from the secret; it never sees the secret itself, a device name, a file name or a file. Every message through it is signed with the secret, so it can't pretend to be your phone or your PC. Files then go straight between phone and PC, encrypted by WebRTC with keys made on the two devices. When both networks block a direct link, they pass through a relay run by Cloudflare that forwards the encrypted bytes and can't read them. To find its own internet address, ihz.land also asks a public STUN server (Cloudflare's or Google's) when a phone connects. **Remove** a phone in **Paired phones** and the pair stops working on both sides; with no paired phones, nothing is sent at all.


**AI usage, only while the AI tab is open or its notices are on.** The AI tab shows how much is left of the AI coding plans you use on this computer (Codex, Claude Code and the others in the table below). To fill a card, ihz.land reads that service's own sign-in on your computer (the files in the table, or a key you typed) and asks that service's own server how much of your plan is used. It reads when the tab opens and every minute while the tab stays open, and asks each service's server at most every few minutes ([docs/ai-usage.md](docs/ai-usage.md) has each one's pace). If you switch on **Tell me before I run out**, it also reads every 5 minutes while the tab is closed and asks each server at most every 10 minutes, so the island can warn you before a limit runs out; that stops while the computer is locked, the screen is off or battery saver is on. With the tab closed and the notices off, nothing runs.

**Each sign-in goes only to its own service.** A sign-in or key read here is sent only to the server of the service it belongs to (Claude Code's to `api.anthropic.com`, Codex's to `chatgpt.com`, an OpenRouter key to `openrouter.ai`), never to anyone else, and never into the log. Nothing about your usage goes anywhere else: the numbers come from each service straight to your computer and stay in its memory. Each request carries a User-Agent naming ihz.land and its version, never another app's. ihz.land only reads: it never changes another app's files and never renews another app's sign-in, which could sign you out of that app, so when a sign-in has run out, the card asks you to open that app once. On a Mac, a sign-in kept in the keychain is read with macOS's own `security` tool, only while the AI tab is open and at most once each time you open it; if macOS asks you first and you say no, it isn't asked again until you press **Refresh**.

**Keys you type, only for the services that need one.** OpenRouter, Z.ai and MiniMax read your usage with a key you make on their site and type into the AI tab, under **Services**; Kimi Code and OpenCode Go take one too. A key goes only to its own service's servers, as above. Kimi Code, Z.ai and MiniMax each also have a server in mainland China for accounts made there, so a key their other server refuses is tried there too, on each refresh until it works or you remove it. ihz.land keeps a key sealed so only your Windows account on this PC can read it (on a Mac, in a file only your Mac account can read, which Time Machine leaves out of its backups), never in your settings or the log.


**To undo any of it:** hide a service under **Services** and ihz.land stops reading it. **Remove** a key you typed and its sealed copy is deleted; to stop the key working anywhere, delete it on the service's own site too (such as OpenRouter's keys page).

**Add-ons, only the ones you switch on.** Add-ons (the last tab, a "+") are extra features whose files aren't part of the app. Switching one on (in that tab, or Japanese readings from the offer under a Japanese song's lyrics) downloads its files from this project's releases on GitHub, or, for the Downloader's yt-dlp and QuickJS-ng, from those projects' own releases there (`github.com`, which serves them from `release-assets.githubusercontent.com`), the same way your browser downloads the installer. Nothing about you goes with the request beyond what any download carries (your IP address and a User-Agent naming ihz.land). Each file is checked against the size and fingerprint built into the app and refused if it differs. Switching the add-on off deletes its files. While every add-on is off, nothing is downloaded.

**New versions.** About two minutes after it starts and then every hour, ihz.land reads one small file, `ihzaskip.github.io/oyenza-studio/latest.json`, which names the latest version (never while the PC is locked, the screen is off or battery saver is on), and again whenever you pick **Check for updates…** in the menu or **Check now** in Settings. The website is hosted by GitHub Pages. The request carries nothing about you beyond what any page view carries (your IP address, and a User-Agent naming ihz.land and its version), and we get no record of it. The file is signed, and the app ignores it unless the signature checks out. On Windows, once the file names a newer version, the app downloads that version's Setup from this project's releases on GitHub, once, the way add-ons download, and keeps it in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Poni\update` until you install it; it is deleted once you run that version. On a Mac it does the same with the DMG, in `~/Library/Caches/Poni/update`, with the app it takes out of it.

**Prayer times' location, only while that add-on is on.** To pick the nearest kab/kota or city, ihz.land asks Windows where the PC is, through Windows' own location service (Settings › Privacy & security › Location decides whether it may). It asks when the add-on starts and again every few hours, so a trip moves the place. The point is used on your PC to choose the nearest place, then dropped: it isn't saved and ihz.land sends it nowhere. Kemenag's kab/kota are in the add-on; cities outside Indonesia are looked up online, a small file at a time, from `studio.oyenza.com/places/` (the website, hosted by GitHub Pages). Away from Kemenag's places, the location asks for the cities of the 5-degree squares (about 550 km across) around it; typing a place asks for the cities whose names start with the first two letters typed. Those requests carry nothing about you beyond what any page view carries (your IP address, and a User-Agent naming ihz.land and its version), and we get no record of them. Only the chosen place is saved, in your settings: its name, point and clock. Picking a place by hand stops the asking.


**Downloader, only while a download runs.** The Downloader add-on (on Windows and the Mac) saves the video or sound of a link you paste, with yt-dlp, a program that runs on your PC only while a download does. yt-dlp asks the site of that link for its page and downloads the video or sound from wherever that site keeps it. These requests go straight from your PC to the site, as your browser's would: the site sees your IP address, and it has its own terms. They carry none of your browser's cookies or sign-ins, and nothing about the link or the download goes to us or anyone else. Finished files go to your Downloads folder and onto the Shelf; the list of downloads is kept in memory only. Switching the Downloader off deletes yt-dlp and its other tools; the files you downloaded stay.

**IELTS, only while that add-on is on and you practise.** The IELTS tab is a speaking coach: Pulo asks IELTS Speaking questions out loud and listens to your answers. It needs your ihz.app sign-in, and nothing happens until you press **Mulai**. While a session runs, the microphone records only between your taps on the mic (or until the question's time is up). Each answer, as a short WAV recording, goes to [Teman IELTS](https://temanielts.com) (`temanielts.com/island/ielts`) with this PC's ihz.app key; Teman IELTS checks the key with ihz.app, writes Pulo's next turn and your band, and keeps the session in your Teman IELTS speaking history. The recording on your PC is deleted once it's sent.


Nothing else is sent anywhere. Music, volume, Bluetooth battery levels, the date and calendar, and the system stats are all read from Windows on your PC. The Caffeine add-on sends nothing at all: the drinks you add, its settings and its reminder are worked out and kept on your PC.

**Show over games reads Windows' frame log, only while it's on and a game is in front.** To count frames per second, ihz.land reads Windows' own log of the frames apps hand to the screen (event tracing, as PresentMon and Xbox Game Bar do) and keeps only the full-screen game's: when each frame came and which window it was for, the last two seconds of them, in memory. Nothing is loaded into the game, nothing is saved and nothing is sent anywhere. The one-time OK adds your Windows account to Windows' Performance Log Users group, which lets programs you run read Windows' performance logs; remove your account from that group to undo it (see the README).

**The Glass theme reads the screen behind the island, only while it's on.** To bend what's under the island, ihz.land copies the part of your screen behind its window (the island and the space it opens into) whenever something there changes. It doesn't copy while the island is hidden, while a full-screen app is in front, or while the PC is locked. The copy stays in your PC's memory and on its graphics card, and each new copy replaces the last. It is never saved, never shown anywhere else and never sent anywhere. While Glass is on, Windows leaves ihz.land out of screenshots, screen recordings and screen sharing. Switch to Classic or Oyen to stop both.

**Notifications, only in the Microsoft Store copy, and only once you allow it in Windows.** In the Microsoft Store copy, ihz.land can read the notifications other apps show (the app's name and the notification's text). It's on from the start, but nothing is read until you press **Allow** on its tab and say yes to Windows' question. Then it reads each new one as it arrives, shows the ones from the apps you picked on the island for a few seconds, and sends nothing anywhere. Take it back any time in Windows Settings > Privacy & security > Notifications, or switch Notifications off under **Tabs** in Settings. The download from GitHub can't read notifications and has no Notifications.

## What stays on your PC

| What | Where |
|---|---|
| Settings, tasks, focus count, the shelf's list of files (the files themselves stay where they are), and any drinks logged by the Water page of older versions | `%APPDATA%\Poni\` |
| Caffeine, once you switch it on: the drinks you added in the last 14 days (each one's kind, shots or servings, beans, time and mg, and the name of one you typed yourself) and the add-on's settings (bedtime, half-life, the limits and the reminder). Nothing about them is sent anywhere | `%APPDATA%\Poni\` (`data.json` and `settings.json`; on a Mac, `~/Library/Application Support/Poni/`) |
| Lyrics cache and a log file (the log names songs that were looked up, to help fix problems) | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Poni\` |

| Your Apple Music token, if you added one, sealed so only your Windows account on this PC can read it | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Poni\` |
| Your ihz.app sign-in (this PC's key and your account's masked email), if you signed in, sealed so only your Windows account on this PC can read it | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Poni\` |
| Keys you typed in the AI tab, each sealed so only your Windows account on this PC can read it (on a Mac, files only your Mac account can read, in `~/Library/Application Support/Poni/`, left out of Time Machine's backups). The AI tab's numbers are kept in memory only, and the log notes only when a service's reading starts or stops working, never a sign-in or a key | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Poni\` |
| AI tab on a Mac with Antigravity's `agy`: a private folder for each run of `agy`, with two small stand-ins for `open` that keep it from opening your browser, removed when the run ends | Your Mac's temporary folder |
| The Glass theme's copy of the screen behind the island, only while Glass is on | Memory only (the PC's and its graphics card's), replaced by each new copy, never written to disk |

| Files of the add-ons you switched on (Prayer times: the place list and the adzan recording; Japanese readings: the reading dictionary; Downloader: yt-dlp, QuickJS-ng and FFmpeg), deleted when you switch the add-on off | `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Poni\addons\` (on a Mac, `~/Library/Caches/Poni/addons/`) |

| Files you saved with the Downloader | Your Downloads folder, kept when you uninstall |
| Notifications' choices: the names and ids of the apps that sent a notification since it came on, and which ones show. Uninstalling removes the app, its cache, its log, the add-ons' files, the Apple Music token, the paired phones, the AI tab's keys and the ihz.app sign-in. To stop the key working on ihz.app too, use **Disconnect** first.
